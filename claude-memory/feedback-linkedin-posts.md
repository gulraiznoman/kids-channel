For the user's personal LinkedIn posts (2026-10-05, marketing post): keep the caption short (they cut a ~150-word draft as too long) and never put their name on the graphic.

Never frame a post around the user making a mistake ("I almost picked the wrong..."); they rejected that. Frame it as an insight they found. Keep it general marketing (they are a marketer), not pharma-specific (no Rx, doctors). Closing question must be easy and safe to answer publicly; never ask people to reveal business info like their competitors.

**Why:** they want it to read like a real person, not an AI-written thread, and LinkedIn already shows who posted.
**How to apply:** aim for about 60 words or less, a hook, one insight, and a question at the end. Keep brand names and internal figures out (see [[project-oestrogel-brand-plan]]). Same plain-copy taste as [[feedback-inflectiv-copy]].

LinkedIn comments on other people's posts (2026-10-05): the user rejected generic praise ("Love this! All the best 🙌", "Truly inspiring"). What landed: short lines (about 1-2 sentences) that share a small positive opinion on the post's actual point, then a light wish. Give 5 options. On political posts, don't mention politics.
